# Falsification and Limits

## Falsifiers

A learning-transition claim is falsified or downgraded when any applicable condition is observed:

1. No identifiable pre-state or post-state exists.
2. Exposure occurred but no attention, interpretation, action, feedback, or revision evidence exists.
3. Behavior changed only through coercion, hidden constraint, reward hacking, memorization, or identity capture.
4. The claimed outcome cannot be distinguished from ordinary measured change.
5. Human consent, agency, responsibility, or contextual authority is absent where required.
6. AI execution is attributed human consent, identity, standing, or accountability by inheritance.
7. Evidence is stale, unverifiable, internally contradictory, or not bound to the transition.
8. The validator input vocabulary passes while the underlying learning claim remains unsupported.
9. A consumer treats repository-local conformance as AE admissibility, execution authority, publication authority, certification, or release approval.

## Limits

The repository can validate declared stage and outcome structures. It cannot by itself establish:

- subjective understanding;
- consciousness or personhood;
- universal learning quality;
- lawful consent in every jurisdiction;
- final AE admissibility;
- execution, deployment, publication, certification, or release authority;
- that a measured improvement will persist outside the observed context;
- that human and AI learning share identical authority or responsibility models.

## Required interpretation

Validator success is necessary structural evidence only. `ADMISSIBLE_DEVELOPMENT` is a bounded candidate classification under the declared model, not a universal or cross-repository judgment. Consumers must retain counterevidence, preserve human/AI distinctions, and use separately admitted integration tasks.
